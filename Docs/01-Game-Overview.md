# Game Overview

## Game Title

**[Working Title]**

---

## Game Genre

**Psychological Puzzle-Adventure**

The game combines exploration, environmental puzzles, memory reconstruction, and emotional decision-making.

There is no combat. The primary gameplay focuses on understanding the environment, interacting with objects, discovering memories, and confronting the player's emotions.

---

## Game Overview

The game follows a young man who has a painful argument with his parents, particularly his father.

Overwhelmed by anger and frustration, he leaves home and gets into a car accident.

He survives the accident but falls into a coma.

Inside the coma, his mind creates a strange psychological world based on his memories, emotions, guilt, and regrets. The world takes the form of distorted versions of familiar places, including a hospital, his family home, his father's office, and other memory spaces.

The player knows from the beginning that the protagonist is in a coma. The mystery is not whether he is dreaming, but **why his mind has trapped him inside these memories and what he needs to confront in order to wake up.**

Throughout the game, the player explores these environments and gradually reconstructs what happened before the accident.

The protagonist initially sees the argument mainly from his own perspective. As he progresses, he begins to understand his father's perspective and realizes that both of them contributed to the conflict in different ways.

The game ultimately leads the protagonist toward acceptance rather than changing or erasing the past.

---

## Main Objective

The player's main objective is to:

**Understand what happened, confront the memories surrounding the accident, accept the past, and wake from the coma.**

The player progresses by:

- Exploring environments.
- Inspecting important objects.
- Discovering Memory Echoes.
- Solving environmental puzzles.
- Reconstructing memories.
- Experiencing Flashbacks.
- Making emotional choices.
- Managing Emotional Pressure.
- Discovering optional Collectables.
- Understanding different perspectives of the same events.

---

## Narrative Structure

The game is divided into five main levels.

Each level represents a different stage of the protagonist's emotional journey.

| Level   | Location | Main Theme | Main Purpose |
| Level 1 | Hospital That Feels Wrong | Confusion | Introduce the coma and core gameplay |
| Level 2 | The Last Evening | Memory | Reconstruct the events before the accident |
| Level 3 | The Father's Office | Understanding | Discover the father's perspective |
| Level 4 | The Conversation | Acceptance | Confront the emotional conflict |
| Level 5 | The Wake Up | Resolution | Return to reality and reconcile with family |

The levels gradually shift from discovering **what happened** to understanding **why it happened** and finally deciding **what to do with it**.

# Level Overview

## Level 1 — The Hospital That Feels Wrong

### Theme

**Confusion, fear, and realization**

The player wakes up in a strange hospital environment after the accident.

The hospital looks familiar but feels wrong. There are no doctors, nurses, or other patients.

The player explores the room and begins noticing objects connected to his identity and recent memories.

The player eventually finds a hospital bracelet with his own name and realizes that he is the patient.

This establishes the central situation:

**The protagonist is in a coma.**

### Gameplay Focus

- Exploration.
- Basic movement and interaction.
- Object inspection.
- Memory Echoes.
- Environmental puzzle.
- Introduction to Emotional Pressure.

### Main Puzzle

The player discovers three symbols representing:

- Memory.
- Accident.
- Coma.

The symbols gradually become clearer as the player investigates important objects.

The player must understand the sequence:

**Photo → Clock → Bracelet**

Representing:

**Before → Accident → Coma**

Solving the puzzle opens the symbolic hospital door.

---

## Level 2 — The Last Evening

### Theme

**Memory and reconstruction**

The player enters a distorted version of the family home.

The goal is to reconstruct the sequence of events that occurred before the accident.

Instead of directly showing the argument, the player discovers fragments of the evening and determines where they belong based on environmental clues.

### Gameplay Focus

- Exploration.
- Environmental evidence.
- Memory reconstruction.
- Memory Echoes.
- Short memory sequences.
- Environmental storytelling.

### Main Puzzle

The player places four memory fragments into their correct locations.

The sequence is:

1. Father arrives home.
2. Conversation begins.
3. Argument escalates.
4. Boy leaves the house.

Correct placement triggers a short memory sequence.

Incorrect placement causes a brief distortion and small increase in Emotional Pressure.

The final memory reveals the protagonist leaving the house immediately before the accident.

The level ends with the realization that the argument was the final event before the crash.

---

## Level 3 — The Weight of the Father

### Theme

**Understanding and perspective**

The player enters the father's office.

Unlike previous levels, the entire level takes place inside this single environment.

The player explores the office and discovers evidence of the father's responsibilities, including work, financial concerns, family responsibilities, and concern for his son.

The protagonist begins to understand that his father's behavior came partly from worry and responsibility, even though the way he expressed it created conflict.

### Gameplay Focus

- Environmental exploration.
- Object inspection.
- Memory reconstruction.
- Memory Echoes.
- Environmental storytelling.

### Main Puzzle

The player discovers distorted memory fragments and connects them to appropriate objects or areas inside the office.

The memories reveal:

- The father's work responsibilities.
- Financial pressure.
- His responsibility toward the family.
- His concern for his son.

The level ends with the realization:

> "He was worried... But he never knew how to say it."

This does not excuse the father's behavior, but gives the protagonist a more complete understanding of the conflict.

---

## Level 4 — The Conversation

### Theme

**Acceptance**

Level 4 is the most psychological and longest level.

The player enters a surreal version of the father's office and finally confronts a representation of his father.

Instead of solving a traditional environmental puzzle, the main gameplay revolves around a conversation.

The player is presented with emotional choices throughout the conversation.

Choices influence:

- Dialogue.
- Short memory responses.
- Visual presentation.
- Emotional Pressure.

However, the major story progression remains linear and all choices eventually converge toward the same conclusion.

This keeps the system manageable while allowing the player to express how they interpret the events.

### Gameplay Focus

- Dialogue choices.
- Emotional decision-making.
- Memory responses.
- Emotional Pressure.
- Psychological distortion.
- Major Flashbacks.

The player eventually reaches the central question:

**"Do you want to change what happened?"**

The player cannot actually change the past.

Instead, the conversation leads the protagonist to understand that acceptance does not mean approving everything that happened.

It means acknowledging what happened and deciding what to do afterward.

The father eventually disappears and tells the protagonist:

> "You don't need me here anymore."

The player then hears the sound of a hospital monitor.

A voice calls out:

> "Wake up."

The screen fades to black.

---

## Level 5 — The Wake Up

### Theme

**Resolution and reconciliation**

The protagonist wakes up in a real hospital.

The surreal world is gone.

The hospital is now realistic and stable.

The player discovers that the father survived and has been sitting beside the protagonist while waiting for him to wake up.

The protagonist remembers the events that led to the accident and understands the emotional journey he experienced inside the coma.

His father is alive.

His mother is also present.

The protagonist apologizes to his parents for the things he said and for leaving the house while overwhelmed by anger.

The parents acknowledge the conflict rather than pretending that everything was perfect.

The protagonist finally expresses that he wants to go home.

The game ends with the idea that:

**The past cannot be changed, but what happens next can still be chosen.**
