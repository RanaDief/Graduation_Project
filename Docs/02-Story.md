# Story

The game starts with a young man who gets into a serious car accident after an intense argument with his parents.

He survives the accident but falls into a coma.

Inside the coma, his mind creates a strange psychological world that resembles a hospital, his family home, and distorted versions of places connected to his memories.

The player knows from the beginning that the boy is in a coma. His goal is to understand what happened before the accident and confront the emotions surrounding it so that he can wake up.

As he explores the psychological world, he begins recovering fragments of his past. Some memories are painful, while others are warm and comforting. Together, these memories gradually reveal that the argument was more complicated than he originally remembered.

The story focuses on **anger, guilt, misunderstanding, regret, perspective, acceptance, and forgiveness.**

---

# Before the Accident

Before the accident, the boy's relationship with his parents had become increasingly strained.

His father had high expectations for him and often tried to guide him, but the boy interpreted these attempts as criticism and pressure.

The boy felt that his father did not understand him or appreciate what he was trying to accomplish.

His frustration had been building for some time.

At the same time, his father was dealing with his own responsibilities and worries. His attempts to protect and guide his son often came across as controlling or dismissive.

Neither side fully understood what the other was feeling.

Despite this tension, the family still had many positive memories together.

The boy's memories contain moments of:

* Family dinners.
* Laughter.
* Conversations.
* His parents supporting him.
* His father spending time with him.
* Ordinary moments that once felt unimportant.

These memories become important later because they show that the relationship cannot be defined only by the argument.

---

# The Argument

On the night of the accident, an argument between the boy and his parents reaches its breaking point.

The argument begins with something relatively ordinary but quickly becomes more emotional because of the frustrations that have accumulated over time.

The boy feels that his parents, especially his father, are not listening to him.

His father believes he is trying to help his son avoid making a mistake.

Instead of communicating these feelings clearly, both sides become defensive.

The conversation turns into an argument.

The boy says things he does not fully mean.

His father responds from frustration rather than understanding.

The argument becomes increasingly intense.

The boy eventually reaches his emotional limit and says something that deeply hurts his family.

He decides to leave.

His parents try to stop him, but he refuses to continue the conversation.

He leaves the house while still overwhelmed with anger.

---

# The Accident

The boy gets into his car and drives away while emotionally overwhelmed.

His mind continues replaying the argument.

The details of what happens on the road are fragmented.

There are flashes of:

* Headlights.
* The steering wheel.
* The road.
* Screeching tires.
* A sudden impact.

The accident occurs.

The boy loses consciousness.

---

# The Coma

The boy survives the accident but falls into a coma.

His body is taken to the hospital.

In reality, his family is there waiting for him to wake up.

However, the boy's consciousness creates its own version of the hospital.

Inside his coma, he wakes up in an empty hospital room and that he is trapped inside a coma.

He understands that he needs to wake up.

However, his mind cannot simply return to reality.

The memories surrounding the accident are incomplete, distorted, and emotionally overwhelming.

To move forward, he must confront them.

---

# The Psychological World

The world inside the coma is a distorted representation of the boy's memories and emotions.

It begins with a hospital because the hospital represents his current physical state.

As the story progresses, the environment begins shifting between places connected to his memories.

The hospital can transform into:

* His family's living room.
* Familiar areas of his home.
* Distorted versions of real locations.
* Abstract representations of his emotional state.

The world does not follow normal physical rules.

Rooms can connect to memories that happened years earlier.

Objects can contain fragments of conversations.

Familiar places can become distorted when the boy is unable to confront what they represent.

The psychological world is not trying to physically imprison him.

It represents the emotional conflict that he has not yet resolved.

---

# The Boy's Emotional Journey

The boy initially remembers the argument primarily through his own perspective.

He remembers feeling:

* Angry.
* Ignored.
* Pressured.
* Misunderstood.
* Hurt.

As he progresses through the psychological world, he begins discovering that his memories are incomplete.

He starts seeing moments that challenge his original interpretation of the argument.

He remembers the good moments with his family.

He begins understanding why his father behaved the way he did.

This does not mean that everything his father did was correct.

Instead, the boy begins recognizing that both sides were dealing with their own emotions and that the argument was the result of accumulated frustration and misunderstanding.

Eventually, the boy is forced to confront the things he said and did himself.

He realizes that being hurt does not mean that he was incapable of hurting others.

---

# Story Progression

## Level 1 — The Hospital That Feels Wrong

The boy wakes up in the psychological hospital after the accident.

He initially panics, believing he is still in danger.

After calming down, he begins exploring the room.

He discovers a hospital bracelet lying on the floor with his own name on it.

This allows him to understand that he is the patient and that he is currently in a coma.

He remembers fragments of the accident but cannot remember everything that happened before it.

While exploring the room, he encounters objects connected to his memories.

A broken family photograph reveals a positive memory.

A frozen clock recalls the accident.

Other environmental interactions reveal fragments of the argument.

The boy begins to understand that his memories are somehow shaping the world around him.

After solving the first puzzle, he leaves the hospital room and enters a strange corridor leading toward a familiar part of his home.

The level establishes the main objective:

> **If he wants to wake up, he needs to understand what happened.**

---
# Level 2 — The Last Evening

## Overview

Level 2 takes place inside a distorted version of the family's house.

The player explores the house and reconstructs the events of the final evening before the accident.

Environmental objects provide clues about where each memory happened.

The main goal is to understand how the normal conversation gradually became an argument and eventually led to the boy leaving the house.

The level establishes:

- The argument developed gradually.
- The boy's memories are incomplete.
- Objects can trigger memories.
- The accident happened shortly after the boy left.
- The boy still does not understand his father's perspective.

---

# Level Flow

## Phase 1 — Entering the House

### Starting State

The player walks through the hospital corridor from Level 1.

The hospital slowly changes into the family's house.

The player enters the house.

The environment looks familiar but feels empty and slightly distorted.

Possible dialogue:

> "This is my house..."

Pause.

> "Why am I here?"

The player begins exploring.

---

# Phase 1 — Exploration

The house contains:

- Main entrance.
- Living room.
- Kitchen.
- Hallway.
- Boy's bedroom.
- Front door.

Important objects include:

- Father's work bag.
- Two cups.
- Boy's headphones.
- Family photograph.
- Television.
- Telephone.
- Front door.

---

## Interaction 1 — Father's Work Bag

The player finds his father's work bag near the entrance.

When inspected, the player hears a door opening.

A short memory appears.

### Memory

The father enters the house with his work bag.

He puts it near the entrance and looks toward the living room.

The memory ends.

Possible dialogue:

> "He had just gotten home..."

---

## Interaction 2 — Kitchen Table

Two cups are placed on the kitchen table.

When inspected, the player hears distant voices.

The voices disappear before the conversation can be understood.

Possible dialogue:

> "We were talking here..."

This provides a clue that part of the evening happened in the kitchen.

---

## Interaction 3 — Boy's Headphones

The player's headphones are lying in his bedroom.

When inspected, distorted music plays.

The player hears his own voice:

> "You never listen to me!"

The sound cuts out.

Possible dialogue:

> "I remember being angry..."

---

## Interaction 4 — Front Door

The front door is slightly open.

Cold air enters the house.

The player hears footsteps outside.

The sound disappears.

Possible dialogue:

> "I left..."

# Memory Echoes

Memory Echoes are fragments of the past that appear through environmental interactions.

They do not directly increase Emotional Pressure.

They provide:

- Story information.
- Puzzle clues.
- Dialogue fragments.
- Additional family memories.

They are not collectibles.

---

## Echo 1 — Television

The television suddenly turns on.

Static fills the screen.

A distorted voice plays:

> "You need to understand..."

The television turns off.

---

## Echo 2 — Telephone

The telephone begins ringing.

When the player interacts with it, the ringing stops.

The boy's voice plays:

> "I can't do this anymore."

The phone becomes silent.

---

## Echo 3 — Family Photograph

The player finds a family photograph.

The photograph briefly becomes clear.

A short positive memory plays showing the family laughing together.

The memory disappears.

Possible dialogue:

> "It wasn't always like this..."

---

# Phase 1 Completion

After investigating the important objects, the house begins changing.

The lights flicker.

The television turns on.

Fragments of the argument overlap.

Several distorted visual fragments appear throughout the house.

The fragments represent different moments from the final evening.

The main puzzle begins.

---

# Phase 2 — Memory Reconstruction

The player must place each memory fragment in the location where the event actually happened.

Environmental objects provide the clues.

The player must reconstruct four events:

1. Father arrives.
2. Conversation begins.
3. Argument escalates.
4. Boy leaves.

---

## Memory Fragment 1 — Father Arrives

The work bag near the entrance provides the clue.

**Work Bag = Father Arrives = Main Entrance**

### Correct Placement

The father enters the house, puts down his work bag, and looks toward the living room.

The memory ends.

---

## Memory Fragment 2 — Conversation Begins

The two cups on the kitchen table provide the clue.

**Two Cups = Conversation = Kitchen**

### Correct Placement

The boy and his father sit at the kitchen table.

Father:

> "We need to talk."

Boy:

> "About what?"

The memory ends.

---

## Memory Fragment 3 — Argument Escalates

The headphones in the bedroom provide the clue.

**Headphones = Bedroom = Argument**

### Correct Placement

The boy enters his bedroom.

His father follows.

Father:

> "I'm trying to help you."

Boy:

> "You never listen to me!"

The boy picks up his headphones.

The memory ends.

---

## Memory Fragment 4 — Boy Leaves

The open front door provides the clue.

**Open Door = Leaving = Main Entrance**

### Correct Placement

The boy walks toward the front door.

His father follows.

Boy:

> "I need some space."

The boy leaves.

The door closes.

The memory ends.

---

# Incorrect Placement

If a fragment is placed in the wrong location:

- The room becomes darker.
- Normal ambience disappears.
- The television turns on.
- Static fills the screen.
- A distorted argument fragment plays.
- The memory resets.
- Emotional Pressure increases slightly.

The distorted memory provides additional context instead of simply displaying a "Wrong Answer" message.

---

# Phase 2 Completion

After all four memories are correctly reconstructed, the house becomes silent.

The memories briefly appear together:

- Father arriving.
- Conversation in the kitchen.
- Argument in the bedroom.
- Boy leaving.

The fragments connect into one continuous sequence.

The player realizes that the argument developed gradually.

A final memory appears at the entrance.

---

# Phase 3 — The Final Moment

The final memory activates automatically.

The boy is at the front door.

His father follows him.

Father:

> "Wait."

Boy:

> "Just leave me alone!"

The boy leaves.

The door closes.

Silence.

The memory suddenly shifts.

The player hears:

- Car engine.
- Heavy breathing.
- Screeching tires.
- Car horn.
- Impact.

---

# The Realization

The player returns to the distorted living room.

He looks toward the front door.

He now understands that the argument was the last event before the accident.

Possible dialogue:

> "I left..."

Pause.

> "And then..."

The player realizes that he was still overwhelmed by anger when he left.

However, he still does not understand everything about his father's actions.

He realizes there are more memories he needs to confront.

---

# Emotional Pressure

Memory Echoes do not directly increase Pressure.

Pressure mainly increases through:

- Incorrect memory placements.
- Intense argument memories.
- Prolonged exposure to highly distorted memories.

### Low Pressure

- Stable environment.
- Clear audio.
- Normal lighting.

### Medium Pressure

- Slight visual distortion.
- Louder breathing.
- Lighting flickers.
- Argument fragments become clearer.

### High Pressure

- Unstable lighting.
- Overlapping voices.
- Stronger environmental distortion.
- Louder breathing.

The player should not normally reach 100% Pressure during this level.

---

# Level Ending

The living room becomes silent.

The house slowly disappears.

A hospital corridor appears where the front door was.

The player looks back at the house.

A distant voice says:

> "Wait."

The player turns around.

Nobody is there.

He continues walking into the corridor.

The screen fades to black.

**Level 2 Complete.**

# Level 3 — The Weight of the Father

## Overview

The player enters a distorted version of the family home after reconstructing the events of the final evening.

This level shifts the perspective from the son's experience to the father's life and responsibilities.

The goal is not to excuse the father's behavior, but to help the player understand what may have influenced his actions.

The player discovers evidence of:

- Work pressure
- Financial responsibility
- Family responsibilities
- Concern for his son

The level ends with the player seeing the argument from the father's perspective.

---

# Level Flow

## Phase 1 — Entering the Memory

The player enters the family home.

The environment is familiar but distorted:

- Slightly darker lighting
- Unstable shadows
- Clocks behaving strangely
- Distant sounds from the father's workplace
- Objects appearing displaced
- Occasional memory distortions

The house contains several areas:

- Living room
- Father's workspace
- Boy's bedroom
- Hallway
- Main entrance

The player begins exploring the environment.

---

# Phase 2 — Discovering the Father's Life

The player investigates objects around the house.

Important environmental evidence includes:

- Work documents
- Bills
- Work tools
- Father's jacket
- Family photographs
- Telephone
- Keys
- Clock

These objects reveal information about the father's responsibilities without directly explaining everything through dialogue.

---

# Memory Fragment Mechanic

Memory fragments appear as distorted pieces of reality rather than normal collectible objects.

They look like:

- Broken pieces of a past scene
- Translucent visual distortions
- Flickering fragments
- Small pieces of the environment mixed with the memory

The player does not simply pick them up and carry them.

Instead:

1. The player discovers a memory fragment.
2. The player investigates it.
3. The player identifies the location it belongs to.
4. The player approaches that location.
5. The fragment reacts when close to the correct place.
6. The player interacts with it.
7. The fragment aligns with the environment.
8. The environment temporarily transforms into the memory.
9. A short memory sequence plays.
10. The world returns to its distorted state.

This makes the mechanic feel like reconstructing a memory rather than collecting items.

---

# Phase 3 — Reconstructing the Father's Memories

The player must align four important memories with their correct locations.

## Memory 1 — Work

### Evidence

Work-related documents and tools.

### Correct Location

Father's desk/workspace.

### Memory

The father is shown working late and dealing with work responsibilities.

The scene establishes that work occupies a significant part of his life.

---

## Memory 2 — Financial Responsibility

### Evidence

Bills and financial documents.

### Correct Location

Father's desk/workspace.

### Memory

The father is shown dealing with bills and household expenses.

The scene reinforces the pressure of supporting the family.

---

## Memory 3 — Family Responsibility

### Evidence

Family photographs and personal belongings.

### Correct Location

Living room.

### Memory

A short positive family memory plays.

The scene reminds the player that the father was not defined only by conflict.

He also had a relationship with his family and cared about them.

---

## Memory 4 — Concern for His Son

### Evidence

The boy's belongings and family photographs.

### Correct Location

Boy's bedroom.

### Memory

The father is shown checking on or thinking about his son.

The scene suggests that his behavior during the argument was partly driven by concern.

However, the game does not present this concern as justification for hurting his son emotionally.

---

# Environmental Storytelling

The player gradually understands the father's situation through the environment.

## Work Desk

- Documents
- Work equipment
- Notes
- Telephone

## House

- Bills
- Family photographs
- Everyday responsibilities

## Boy's Room

- Personal belongings
- Signs of the son's life
- Evidence that the father pays attention to him

The player should understand the father's pressure without needing long exposition.

---

# Phase 4 — Final Memory

After all four memories have been reconstructed, the environment becomes increasingly unstable.

The player is taken back to the final argument.

This time, the event is presented from the father's perspective.

The player sees:

- The father trying to communicate
- The father's frustration
- His concern about his son
- The son's emotional reaction
- The argument escalating
- The son deciding to leave

The same event from Level 2 is now given additional context.

---

# Important Narrative Point

The level should not communicate:

> "The father was right."

Instead, it communicates:

> "The father was worried, but he did not know how to express it."

The player can understand the father's intentions while still recognizing that his actions and words caused emotional harm.

---

# Emotional Pressure

Emotional Pressure increases during the more intense parts of the level.

Pressure can rise from:

- Confronting painful memories
- Watching the final argument
- Remaining inside highly distorted areas
- Repeatedly failing important interactions

Memory fragments themselves do **not** automatically increase Pressure.

Exploring memories is safe.

The emotional confrontation is what creates pressure.

---

# Pressure States

## Low Pressure

- Environment mostly stable
- Clear audio
- Normal lighting

## Medium Pressure

- Lighting flickers
- Audio becomes slightly distorted
- Shadows become unstable

## High Pressure

- Voices overlap
- Heartbeat and breathing become louder
- Environment becomes increasingly distorted
- Memory scenes become harder to perceive clearly

## Maximum Pressure

The memory becomes completely unstable.

The screen fades or the environment collapses, returning the player to the latest checkpoint/current section.

---

# Level Completion

After witnessing the final memory, the player understands that the argument was more complicated than initially believed.

The final realization is:

> **"He was worried... But he never knew how to say it."**

The environment begins transitioning away from the family home.

A distorted hospital corridor begins to appear, leading into **Level 4 — The Memory Timeline**.
