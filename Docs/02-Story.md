# Story

## Overview

The game follows a young man who is involved in a car accident after a painful argument with his father.

Before the main events of the game begin, the protagonist is in a hospital room beside his injured father. Feeling regret about the argument and emotionally overwhelmed by what happened, he lies down on the couch beside his father and falls asleep.

While sleeping, he experiences a vivid dream-like memory of the accident. He relives the argument with his father, leaves the house on a dark and rainy night, and is nearly hit by a speeding car while crossing the street. His father pushes him out of the way and takes the impact himself.

The protagonist wakes from the nap and begins exploring a distorted psychological world connected to his memories, emotions, guilt, and regrets. As he progresses, he must understand what happened between him and his father and why the memories continue to affect him.

The protagonist does not fully understand the meaning of everything he remembers at first. The story gradually reveals the events that led to the accident and the different perspectives behind the conflict.

---

## Main Conflict

The protagonist believes that his father did not understand him and that the argument was caused by his father's pressure and lack of communication.

His guilt becomes stronger after remembering that his father pushed him out of the way of the car and took the impact himself.

As he explores his memories, he gradually discovers another perspective.

His father was dealing with work, financial responsibilities, and concern for his son. However, his attempts to help often came across as pressure and control.

The protagonist begins to understand that neither side was completely right or wrong.

---

## Story Progression

The story follows the protagonist through four emotional stages:

**Remember → Understand → Confront → Accept**

### Level 1 — The Hospital That Feels Wrong

The protagonist begins beside his injured father in a hospital room. Feeling regret about their argument, he falls asleep on the couch beside his father.

During his sleep, he experiences a vivid memory of the accident. He remembers the argument, leaving the house on a dark and rainy night, crossing the street, and nearly being hit by a car. His father pushes him away and takes the hit himself.

The protagonist wakes from the nap and begins the playable part of the level. He then discovers that the hospital and the strange world around him are connected to his memories.

### Level 2 — The Last Evening

The protagonist explores deeper into the memories surrounding the evening before the accident.

He begins to understand what led to the argument with his father, including their conversation, the tension between them, and the emotions that caused him to leave the house.

The memories reveal details that were missing from the first accident memory and give the protagonist a clearer understanding of what happened before he left.

### Level 3 — The Weight of the Father

The protagonist explores memories connected to his father.

He discovers his father's responsibilities, worries, and concern for the family.

He begins to realize that his father's actions came partly from fear and concern, even though they caused conflict.

### Level 4 — The Conversation

The protagonist confronts a psychological representation of his father.

Through dialogue and emotional choices, he revisits the argument and considers both perspectives.

He realizes that he cannot change what happened.

Instead, he must accept it and decide what to do next.

The father disappears after telling him:

> "You don't need me here anymore."

The protagonist then hears a hospital monitor and his father's voice:

> "Wake up."

### Level 5 — The Wake Up

The protagonist wakes up in the real hospital.

He discovers that his father survived and has been waiting beside him.

His mother is also present.

The protagonist remembers everything and apologizes to his parents for the argument, the things he said, and leaving while overwhelmed by anger.

His father acknowledges that he also made mistakes.

The protagonist finally says that he wants to go home.

---

## Ending

The game ends with the protagonist awake in the real world, reunited with his parents.

The psychological world was created from his memories, guilt, regret, and unresolved emotions while he was in a coma.

He cannot change the accident or erase the argument.

What he can change is what he does after waking up.

### Final Theme

**The past cannot be changed, but we can choose what happens next.**

# Level 1 — The Hospital That Feels Wrong

## Overview

Level 1 is the player's introduction to the psychological world.

The player begins in a hospital room beside his father, who is lying injured on a hospital bed after the accident. The player feels regret about the argument he had with his father before the accident. Exhausted and emotionally overwhelmed, he lies down on the couch beside his father and falls asleep.

While sleeping, the player experiences a dream-like memory of the accident. He relives the argument with his father, leaves the house on a dark and rainy night, and is nearly hit by a speeding car while crossing the street. His father pushes him out of the way and takes the impact himself.

The player wakes up from the nap and begins the playable part of the level. The level then introduces the connection between the hospital and his memories.

The main goal of the level is to establish:

- The player has a strong emotional connection to his father.
- The argument before the accident is an important part of the player's memories.
- The accident is connected to the player's painful memories.
- His memories are connected to the world around him.
- There are both positive and painful memories.
- The player must explore and confront his memories to progress.

---

# Level Flow

## Phase 1 — Hospital Opening

### Starting State

The game begins inside a hospital room.

The player is sitting beside his father, who is lying injured on a hospital bed after the accident.

The player is not initially controllable.

The player can hear:

- The father's breathing.
- A faint heartbeat and heart monitor.
- Rain hitting the hospital window.
- Distant hospital ambience.
- A quiet electrical hum.

The room is quiet and emotionally heavy.

The player looks at his father and feels regret about what happened between them.

Possible dialogue:

> "I'm sorry, Dad..."

The player remains beside his father for a moment. He is exhausted and emotionally overwhelmed.

There is a couch beside the hospital bed.

The player sits down on the couch and looks at his father one more time.

He lies down on the couch and slowly falls asleep.

### Accident Dream / Memory

The screen slowly fades to black.

The player hears:

- Heavy rain.
- Thunder.
- Distant traffic.
- Heavy breathing.

The player opens his eyes and finds himself back at home.

It is a dark and rainy night.

The player is standing inside the house with his father.

He is reliving the argument that happened before the accident.

The argument becomes more intense.

Possible dialogue:

> "You never listen to me."

The player responds angrily.

> "Because you never listen to me either!"

The argument ends with the player deciding to leave the house.

His father tries to stop him.

> "Wait!"

The player ignores him and leaves.

### The Accident

The player walks outside into the dark, rainy night.

Rain falls heavily and the streets are wet.

The player walks toward the street while still angry and distracted by the argument.

He begins crossing the street.

A car suddenly approaches at high speed.

The player hears a loud horn and turns toward the car.

The car is about to hit him.

Everything slows down.

His father runs toward him and pushes him out of the car's path.

The player falls away from the road.

His father is hit by the car.

The player sees the impact.

The screen flashes white.

The sound suddenly disappears.

The memory ends.

### Waking Up

The player suddenly wakes up on the hospital couch.

His breathing becomes heavy.

He looks around the room in confusion.

He immediately looks toward his father on the hospital bed.

The player slowly realizes that the accident he just experienced was a memory of what happened.

He looks at his father with guilt and regret.

The player stands up from the couch.

The player is now fully in control and the playable part of Level 1 begins.

---

# Phase 1 — Exploration

The player is now free to explore the hospital room.

The room initially appears normal.

The environment contains:

- Hospital bed.
- Side table.
- Broken family photograph.
- Hospital bracelet.
- Wall clock.
- Telephone.
- Television.
- Locked hospital door.
- Other ordinary hospital equipment.

There are no doctors, nurses, or other patients.

The room feels unusually empty.

The player can interact with the important objects.

---

## Interaction 1 — Hospital Bracelet

The player notices a hospital bracelet lying on the floor beside the bed.

The bracelet appears damaged and slightly dirty.

The player picks it up.

The name printed on the bracelet catches his attention.

It is his own name.

The player looks at it again.

Possible dialogue:

> "That's my name..."

He looks toward the hospital bed.

He realizes that the bracelet belongs to him.

A short pause.

> "I'm the patient."

The player begins putting the pieces together.

He remembers being unconscious.

He realizes:

> "I'm in a coma."

This establishes the player's main objective:

> **Wake up.**

The player now understands that he survived the accident but is currently unconscious.

However, he still does not know exactly what happened.

---

## Interaction 2 — Broken Family Photo

The player discovers a broken family photograph.

The photograph shows the boy with his parents.

The glass is cracked across the image.

When the player picks it up, the photograph briefly becomes clear.

The cracks disappear.

The player experiences a short positive memory.

### Memory

The boy is with his family at home.

They are laughing together.

The memory is warm and peaceful.

There is no dialogue about the accident or the argument.

The memory lasts several seconds.

The scene suddenly disappears.

The player is back inside the hospital.

The photograph is broken again.

Possible dialogue:

> "I remember this..."

This is the player's first positive memory.

The player learns that the hospital is somehow connected to his memories.

---

## Interaction 3 — Frozen Clock

The player notices a clock on the wall.

The clock is frozen at a specific time.

The player interacts with it.

The second hand suddenly starts moving.

The hands begin moving backward.

The clock stops at the same time again.

The room becomes silent.

The player hears:

- Screeching tires.
- A car horn.
- A loud impact.

A flash of headlights briefly fills the room.

The player sees a fragmented image of the road.

The memory ends.

The clock returns to its original state.

Possible dialogue:

> "That's when it happened..."

The player now understands that the clock represents the time of the accident.

---

# Memory Echoes

After the player has investigated the three main objects, fragments of the past begin appearing throughout the hospital room.

These are called **Memory Echoes**.

Memory Echoes do not directly increase Emotional Pressure.

Their purpose is to reveal information about the player's past and gradually reconstruct the events that happened before the accident.

They can also provide clues for puzzles.

The player is encouraged to investigate them.

---

## Echo 1 — Television

The player approaches the television.

The screen suddenly turns on.

Static fills the screen.

A distorted voice plays.

> "You need to understand..."

The voice becomes unclear.

The television turns off.

Nothing else happens.

The player has discovered a fragment of the argument.

---

## Echo 2 — Phone

The player approaches the phone.

The phone suddenly begins vibrating.

There is no incoming call.

The player interacts with it.

A fragment of the boy's voice plays:

> "I can't do this anymore."

The phone screen turns black.

---

## Echo 3 — Hospital Door

The player approaches the locked hospital door.

For a moment, voices can be heard from the other side.

The voices are distant and distorted.

The player hears:

> "Just listen..."

Then:

> "I'm done..."

The voices suddenly disappear.

The player opens the door.

The corridor outside is empty.

The player closes the door.

---

# Phase 1 Completion

After the player has:

- Found the hospital bracelet.
- Discovered that he is in a coma.
- Inspected the broken family photo.
- Experienced the positive memory.
- Inspected the frozen clock.
- Experienced the accident memory.
- Discovered the Memory Echoes. (optional)

the room becomes completely silent.

The player returns to the center of the room.

The hospital environment slowly changes.

The lighting becomes warmer.

The player experiences another short positive memory.

This memory shows the boy and his family sharing an ordinary happy moment.

The memory ends abruptly.

The player returns to the hospital.

The room is cold again.

The player now understands that the hospital is connected to his memories.

---

# Phase 2 — The Main Puzzle

After the positive memory ends, the locked hospital door changes.

Three symbols appear on the door.

The symbols represent:

- Memory.
- Accident.
- Coma.

The player must determine how these three concepts are connected.

The objects previously discovered provide the necessary clues.

---

## Puzzle Clue 1 — Family Photo

The family photo represents the boy's life before the accident.

It represents the memories that existed before everything changed.

**Photo = Before**

---

## Puzzle Clue 2 — Clock

The frozen clock represents the moment when the accident happened.

**Clock = Accident**

---

## Puzzle Clue 3 — Hospital Bracelet

The hospital bracelet confirms the player's current state.

It represents what happened after the accident.

**Bracelet = Coma**

---

# Puzzle Solution

The player must reconstruct the sequence:

**Before → Accident → Coma**

The corresponding symbols must therefore be activated in this order:

**1. Family Photo**

**2. Clock**

**3. Hospital Bracelet**

When the player activates the correct symbol:

- The symbol remains active.
- A short sound confirms the interaction.

If the player activates the wrong symbol:

- The symbol flickers.
- A distorted sound plays.
- The sequence resets.
- Emotional Pressure increases slightly.

The penalty is intentionally small so the player can experiment without being heavily punished since it's the first level.

---

# Puzzle Completion

After the player activates the symbols in the correct order:

**Photo → Clock → Bracelet**

the three symbols connect.

The door unlocks.

For a moment, the player hears another fragment of the argument.

The voices are clearer than before.

> "Just listen..."

> "I don't want to..."

The voices stop.

The door opens.

---

# Emotional Pressure

Emotional Pressure is introduced during Level 1 but is not the main challenge of the level.

The player learns that emotionally intense memories can affect their psychological state.

Memory Echoes themselves do not increase Pressure.

Pressure is affected by more intense memories, such as the accident flashback.

### Low Pressure

- Stable environment.
- Clear audio.
- Normal lighting.

### Medium Pressure

- Slight visual distortion.
- Increased breathing.
- Occasional lighting changes.
- Memories become more intense.

The player should not normally reach 100% Pressure during Level 1.

---

# Level Ending

The player walks through the hospital door.

A long, dark corridor is revealed.

The corridor looks much larger than the hospital room should allow.

The player looks behind them.

The hospital room is still visible.

The player turns forward again.

At the end of the corridor is a familiar-looking door.

It resembles the entrance to the family's living room.

The player approaches it.

As he gets closer, the hospital environment begins fading away.

The screen slowly fades to black.

**Level 1 Complete.**
