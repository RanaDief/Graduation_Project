# Level 4 — The Conversation

## Overview

Level 4 is the psychological climax of the game.

After understanding what happened in Level 2 and experiencing the father's perspective in Level 3, the boy is finally forced to confront what he actually feels about that night.

The level takes place in a surreal version of the father's office.

The room is mostly empty.

At the center are two chairs and a small table.

The boy sits opposite his father.

The father represents the boy's memory of him rather than necessarily being the real father.

The entire level is built around a series of choices.

The choices affect the conversation and the memories shown to the player, but they do not create different endings.

All paths eventually lead toward the same realization:

> The past cannot be changed, but it can be understood and accepted.

# Level Flow

## Phase 1 — The Conversation Begins

The player enters the father's office.

The office is almost completely empty.

Only a few objects remain:

- Two chairs
- Small table
- Wall clock
- Family photograph
- Father's jacket
- Boy's headphones
- Father's keys

The father is sitting in one of the chairs.

He looks normal.

There is no distortion.

The father looks at the boy.

Possible dialogue:

> "You wanted to talk."

The boy sits opposite him.

The room becomes silent.

The father asks:

> "Why did you leave?"

The first choice appears.

---

# Phase 2 — Choice 1: Why Did You Leave?

## Choice A — "Because you never listened."

### Father's Response

> "Maybe I didn't."

A short memory appears.

The boy remembers trying to explain himself while the father continued talking.

The memory fades.

---

## Choice B — "Because I was angry."

### Father's Response

> "I know."

A short memory appears showing the boy becoming increasingly frustrated during the argument.

The memory fades.

---

## Choice C — "Because I needed to get away."

### Father's Response

> "You felt like you had nowhere else to go."

A short memory appears showing the boy walking toward the front door.

The memory fades.

---

The conversation continues regardless of the choice.

# Phase 3 — Choice 2: The Father's Intentions

The father looks toward the desk.

The office briefly changes into the father's workspace from Level 3.

Work documents and bills appear.

The father asks:

> "Did you really think I didn't care?"

The player receives three choices.

---

## Choice A — "You never showed it."

### Father's Response

> "Maybe I didn't."

The father looks toward the work documents.

> "I thought providing everything you needed was enough."

The memory fades.

---

## Choice B — "I thought you were trying to control me."

### Father's Response

> "Maybe I was trying too hard."

The father looks down.

> "I was afraid of losing control."

The memory fades.

---

## Choice C — "I know you cared."

The father remains silent for a moment.

Then:

> "Then why didn't you stay?"

The memory fades.

---

# Phase 4 — Choice 3: The Argument

The office begins becoming distorted.

The clock starts ticking louder.

The father asks:

> "Do you think I was wrong?"

The player receives three choices.

---

## Choice A — "Yes."

The father looks at the boy.

> "I probably was."

The argument briefly replays.

The father says something hurtful.

The memory fades.

---

## Choice B — "No."

The father remains silent.

After a moment:

> "Then why does it still hurt?"

The room becomes quiet.

The memory fades.

---

## Choice C — "Both of us were."

The father looks at him.

> "Maybe."

The argument briefly replays from both perspectives.

The memory fades.

---

# Phase 5 — Choice 4: The Accident

The office disappears.

The player is suddenly standing on the road from the accident.

The car is nearby.

The father is standing several meters away.

The environment is completely silent.

The father asks:

> "If you could go back..."

Pause.

> "...would you do anything differently?"

The player receives four choices.

---

## Choice A — "I would stay."

The memory changes.

The boy remains inside the house.

The father and son attempt to talk.

The scene becomes calmer.

However, the memory eventually distorts and disappears.

---

## Choice B — "I would listen."

The argument changes.

The boy listens to his father.

The father tries to explain himself.

The scene eventually fades.

---

## Choice C — "I don't know."

The father looks at him.

> "That's okay."

The road becomes silent.

---

## Choice D — "I would still leave."

The father looks surprised.

> "Why?"

The player responds:

> "Because I needed space."

The father pauses.

> "I understand."

The memory fades.

---

# Phase 6 — The Question

Regardless of the previous choices, the player returns to the father's office.

The father is sitting in the chair again.

The room is becoming increasingly distorted.

The father asks:

> "Do you want to change what happened?"

The player receives three choices.

---

## Choice A — "Yes."

The room resets to the beginning of the argument.

The player briefly experiences the scene again.

They attempt to change what happened.

The memory changes.

However, it eventually collapses.

The accident still occurs.

The player returns to the office.

---

## Choice B — "No."

The father asks:

> "Then what do you want?"

The player responds:

> "To understand it."

The room becomes calmer.

---

## Choice C — "I don't know."

The father looks at the boy.

> "Maybe that's what you're here to figure out."

The room becomes quiet.

---

# Phase 7 — Final Choice

The environment begins fading.

The father asks one final question:

> "What do you want to remember?"

The player receives four choices.

---

## Choice A — "The Good."

The player sees a positive memory of the father and son.

They are together during a happier moment.

The father smiles.

The memory is warm and peaceful.

However, the argument briefly appears at the end.

The player realizes:

> The good memories do not erase the bad ones.

---

## Choice B — "The Bad."

The player sees the argument and the accident.

The environment becomes heavily distorted.

The father appears.

He asks:

> "Is that all I was?"

The player remains silent.

The memory fades.

---

## Choice C — "Nothing."

The room becomes completely empty.

The father disappears.

The player is left alone.

After several seconds, the father's voice is heard:

> "You can't forget."

The hospital monitor begins.

---

## Choice D — "Everything."

The player sees the entire sequence.

The good memories.

The father's responsibilities.

The argument.

The boy's anger.

The father's frustration.

The moment the boy leaves.

The accident.

The memories appear together without becoming distorted.

The father stands beside the boy.

He says:

> "That's the truth."

---

# Phase 8 — Acceptance

Regardless of the final choice, the memories eventually disappear.

The father remains standing in front of the boy.

He asks:

> "Do you understand now?"

The boy looks at him.

Possible dialogue:

> "I can't change what happened."

Pause.

> "But I can choose what I do with it."

The father looks at him.

> "Then it's time."

The father begins fading away.

The boy asks:

> "Where are you going?"

The father responds:

> "You don't need me here anymore."

The father disappears.

---

# Phase 9 — The Final Memory

The office becomes completely dark.

The player hears a faint hospital monitor.

**Beep.**

Silence.

**Beep.**

The sound becomes clearer.

The boy hears someone breathing.

A voice says:

> "Come on..."

The boy recognizes the voice.

The screen remains black.

The father says one final word:

> "Wake up."

The screen fades completely to black.

# Emotional Pressure

Level 4 represents the highest Emotional Pressure level before the hospital reveal.

Pressure is affected mainly by the emotional intensity of the choices and memories.

Pressure increases during:

- Replaying the argument
- Revisiting painful memories
- Experiencing the accident
- Attempting to change the past
- Choosing to focus exclusively on painful memories

Pressure decreases when:

- The player acknowledges the father's perspective
- The player accepts conflicting memories
- The player chooses to understand rather than change the past
- The player reaches the final acceptance sequence

## Low Pressure

- Stable environment
- Clear dialogue
- Normal lighting
- Minimal distortion

## Medium Pressure

- Clock ticking becomes louder
- Lighting flickers
- Memories become slightly distorted
- Background sounds become louder

## High Pressure

- Strong environmental distortion
- Overlapping voices
- Heavy breathing
- Increased heartbeat
- Memories become unstable

## Maximum Pressure

The environment collapses.

The screen fades to black.

The player returns to the beginning of the current conversation section.

The player is never permanently punished for making a choice.

---

# Choice Design

The choices are designed to affect the emotional experience rather than create completely different story branches.

Each choice can change:

- Father's response
- Short memory sequence
- Dialogue
- Emotional Pressure
- Visual presentation

The major story progression remains the same.

This keeps the system manageable to implement while still making the player feel responsible for the conversation.

# Core Purpose of Level 4

**Level 1 asks:**

> "Where am I?"

**Level 2 asks:**

> "What happened?"

**Level 3 asks:**

> "What was my father carrying?"

**Level 4 asks:**

> "What do I want to do with what happened?"

The player has already reconstructed the past.

They have already seen the father's perspective.

Now they must decide how they emotionally respond to it.

The choices do not determine a different ending.

They determine how the player experiences the boy's emotions before he finally accepts the past.

The level ends when the boy is finally ready to wake up.

**Level 5 — The Hospital Reveal** begins.
