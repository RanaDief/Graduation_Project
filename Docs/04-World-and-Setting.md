# World & Setting

## Overview

The game takes place across two connected realities:

1. **The Psychological World** — a distorted world created from the protagonist’s memories, emotions, guilt, and regret while he is in a coma.
2. **The Real World** — the hospital where the protagonist is actually unconscious.

The psychological world is the main gameplay environment, while the real world is revealed at the end.

## Psychological World

The psychological world looks familiar but does not behave realistically. Locations are based on places connected to the protagonist’s memories and emotions.

The environment gradually becomes more distorted as the protagonist confronts painful memories. Objects, lighting, sounds, and spaces can change to reflect his emotional state.

### Main Locations

| Level | Location | Purpose |
| **1** | Hospital Room & Corridor | Introduces the coma and establishes the connection between the hospital and memories. |
| **2** | Family Home | Reconstructs the evening of the argument and the events before the accident. |
| **3** | Father’s Office | Reveals the father’s responsibilities, worries, and perspective. |
| **4** | Distorted Office & Road | Allows the protagonist to confront the argument, make choices, and accept what happened. |
| **5** | Real Hospital | Reveals reality and provides the emotional conclusion. |

## Environmental Design

Each location should be **small and focused**, allowing the team to reuse assets while keeping development manageable.

Environments should communicate story through:

- Family photographs
- Personal belongings
- Furniture and everyday objects
- Documents and bills
- Clocks
- Father’s work equipment
- Hospital equipment
- Lighting and environmental changes
- Memory-related objects

The player should be able to understand parts of the story by observing the environment rather than relying entirely on dialogue.

## Psychological Distortion

The psychological world changes according to the protagonist’s emotional state.

Examples include:

- Flickering or changing lights
- Distorted sounds
- Clocks behaving unnaturally
- Objects appearing or disappearing
- Rooms subtly changing
- Repeated or altered environments
- Increasing visual distortion during intense memories
- Transitions between locations that would not be possible in reality

Distortion should remain **controlled and purposeful**. It should support the story rather than make navigation confusing.

## Real World

The real world appears primarily in Level 5.

The hospital is realistic and stable, contrasting with the psychological world.

It contains:

- The protagonist’s hospital bed
- Medical equipment
- Heart monitor
- Hospital bracelet
- Window and door
- Father sitting beside the bed
- Mother entering the room

There are no psychological distortions in this environment. The realistic setting reinforces that the previous locations were created within the protagonist’s mind.

## Visual Direction

The psychological world should feel familiar at first, then gradually become unsettling.

**Early Game:**
- Realistic environments
- Subtle abnormalities
- Minimal distortion

**Middle Game:**
- More noticeable environmental changes
- Stronger visual and audio distortion
- Memories becoming clearer

**Late Game:**
- Strong psychological distortion during confrontation and acceptance
- Increasing instability as the protagonist faces painful memories

**Ending:**
- Clean, realistic hospital environment representing reality

## Audio & Atmosphere

Sound is used heavily to connect environments with memories and emotions.

Examples include:

- Hospital monitor beeps
- Breathing
- Clock ticking
- Distant voices
- TV static
- Door sounds
- Car and traffic sounds
- Distorted versions of familiar sounds
- Silence during emotional moments
- Subtle background music

Audio distortion increases during high emotional pressure and becomes clear again when the protagonist reaches acceptance.

## World Design Principles

- Keep environments small and manageable.
- Reuse assets across levels where possible.
- Give every location a clear emotional purpose.
- Use environmental storytelling instead of excessive exposition.
- Make distortion meaningful and purposeful.
- Avoid unnecessary NPCs and large environments.
- Maintain a clear contrast between the psychological world and reality.
- Make the world feel mature, personal, and believable rather than fantastical or childish.
